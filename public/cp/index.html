Interview track

Target: Google SWE III (L4). The loop is mostly coding, plus Googleyness, and sometimes one system design round. LLD rarely gets its own round, so it gets Fridays only. DBMS, OS and networks come in where design uses them, not as full courses.

Sheet weeks
Striver's SDE sheet in its order, with the 180 sheet's extra graph, DP, sliding window and binary search problems after the matching section. 25 minutes a problem, approach out loud first. Evenings: a CSES and a Codeforces problem on the same topic.
Design
12:30–13:30 on weekdays. Fundamentals first (estimates, networking, caching, databases, sharding, consistency, queues), then case studies from Alex Xu's two books. Fridays: OOP, patterns, C++ concurrency, LLD classics.
Interview mode
Three weeks of unseen timed sets, a coding mock every Saturday, a design mock every Wednesday, behavioural stories on Fridays.
Off days
Festivals, the wedding, travel: mark the day off and the plan moves back. Nothing gets lighter.

    Rules

    1. Before coding, rewrite the statement in one plain line, then work sample 1 by hand. If you can't do both after 15 minutes, read the Note section and the samples again. Understanding the statement is half the problem right now.
    2. Time-box each problem to 45 minutes. Then read the editorial, close it, and code it yourself.
    3. Ask for help in steps, not for the answer. From Claude or the editorial, take one hint at a time: first "which topic is this?", then "what is the key observation?", and only then the solution. Stop reading as soon as you can continue alone.
    4. Tap "needed help" on any problem where you used a hint, the editorial or Claude. It comes back the next day as a re-solve from a blank file. That re-solve is where the learning happens.
    5. Before you type: write the idea as 2–3 comment lines. Estimate the number of states or operations. If it's above about 10⁸, shrink the search first (for example, one queen per row, not one per square).
    6. Before you submit, run four checks. Bounds: multiply n by the max value, and use long long above 2·10⁹. Impossible cases: when is the answer NO or −1? Code vs idea: walk sample 1 through the code by hand and compare it with your comment lines. Edges: try n = 1, all values equal, and the max values.
    7. Second WA on the same problem? Stop guessing. Write the brute force and run stress/stress.sh from your CP folder. Fix the failing case it prints, then resubmit.
    8. Rounds start at 20:05, right after your meeting. Do every one live. If the meeting runs over, register anyway and start late. Skip the round only if work is on fire, then do it virtual in the next 10:45 block.
    9. CSES stuck past 45 minutes: skip it. You're missing a technique. Come back after the next topic.
    10. Write one line per missed idea in the day's log box.
    11. LeetCode daily is a 30-minute cap. It keeps interview patterns warm; it does not move your rating. If a Hard runs past 30 minutes, read the editorial and move on.
    12. Missed a day? Don't catch up. Do today's plan and move on. If the day was a holiday or you were ill, open it and press "Mark as off day": a weekday's study plan moves to the next weekday and everything after it shifts by one. A weekend day just skips its plan; weekends always follow the week your weekdays are on. The day drops out of the totals, and the streak skips it. Undo pulls the plan back. Be honest with it; it's for days you couldn't, not days you didn't.
    13. LeetCode contests come in at stage 1: Weekly on Sunday 08:00 IST, Biweekly on Saturday 20:00 IST. Do Weekly when there is no Codeforces round the night before; skip Biweekly if it clashes with a round.
